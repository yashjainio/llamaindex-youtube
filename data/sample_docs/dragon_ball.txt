Dragon Ball — Series Overview

Overview
Dragon Ball is a Japanese manga series created by Akira Toriyama, serialized in Weekly Shonen Jump from 1984 to 1995. It follows Son Goku from childhood through adulthood, beginning as a martial arts adventure loosely inspired by the Chinese novel Journey to the West and gradually evolving into the science-fiction battle series most fans recognize today, especially in its sequel arcs collectively known as Dragon Ball Z.

Protagonist and abilities
Son Goku is a Saiyan, a member of a nearly extinct alien warrior race, sent to Earth as an infant. His signature technique is the Kamehameha, a concentrated beam of energy fired from cupped hands, taught to him by his martial arts master Master Roshi. Goku's most famous transformation is Super Saiyan, first triggered during his fight against the tyrant Frieza on the planet Namek after witnessing the death of his close friend Krillin — a moment considered one of the most iconic in anime history. Later transformations include Super Saiyan God, Super Saiyan Blue, and Ultra Instinct, each raising the power ceiling of the series further.

Story arcs
The Saiyan Saga introduces Goku's alien heritage and his brother Raditz. The Namek/Frieza Saga sends Goku and allies to planet Namek in a race against Frieza to collect the seven Dragon Balls, which summon the wish-granting dragon Shenron when gathered together. The Cell Saga introduces the bio-engineered android Cell and popularized the "Room of Spirit and Time," a dimension where a day outside passes as a year inside for accelerated training. The Buu Saga concludes the Z era with the ancient evil Majin Buu.

Production
Dragon Ball was animated by Toei Animation across multiple series: Dragon Ball (1986-1989), Dragon Ball Z (1989-1996), Dragon Ball GT (1996-1997), and the later continuation Dragon Ball Super (2015-2018). Toriyama remained closely involved in the franchise's story direction until his passing in 2024, including writing the original story for Dragon Ball Super.

Legacy and cultural impact
Dragon Ball Z is widely credited with popularizing anime in Western markets during the 1990s and 2000s, airing on Toonami and introducing an entire generation to the genre. Its influence on the shonen battle format — power scaling, tournament arcs, transformation sequences — can be traced through nearly every major series that followed, including Naruto and Demon Slayer. The Kamehameha and Super Saiyan transformation remain among the most widely recognized pieces of anime iconography outside Japan.

Themes
Dragon Ball centers on the joy of constant self-improvement — Goku's defining trait is that he trains for the love of getting stronger and testing himself against worthy opponents, rather than for revenge or duty, which distinguishes his motivation from many shonen protagonists who followed.

Supporting characters
Vegeta, the prince of the Saiyans, begins as a villain in the Saiyan Saga and gradually becomes Goku's rival and ally, driven by pride and a refusal to be permanently surpassed. Piccolo, once Goku's enemy, becomes one of his closest allies and a mentor figure to Goku's son Gohan. Bulma, a brilliant inventor, is responsible for the Dragon Radar that makes locating the seven Dragon Balls possible in the first place. Gohan, Goku's eldest son, is revealed during the Cell Saga to have hidden power potentially exceeding his father's own, though he generally prefers scholarly pursuits over fighting.

Trivia
Dragon Ball Z alone spans 291 episodes, and the franchise as a whole (including Dragon Ball, GT, and Super) totals well over 500 episodes across its various series. Frieza's true final form and the reveal of Super Saiyan are frequently cited among the most influential single moments in anime history for how they reshaped fan expectations around power escalation. The franchise has also produced numerous theatrical films, including the Broly movies, which remain fan favorites for their animation quality.

Voice cast
Goku is voiced by Masako Nozawa in the original Japanese version, a role she has held continuously since the mid-1980s across nearly every series and film in the franchise. In the widely broadcast English dub produced by Funimation, Goku is voiced by Sean Schemmel. Vegeta is voiced by Ryo Horikawa in Japanese and Christopher Sabat in English, both closely associated with the character for decades of continuous casting.

Where to watch
Dragon Ball, Dragon Ball Z, and Dragon Ball Super are available on Crunchyroll, with the Funimation-produced English dub also distributed through Crunchyroll following Funimation's merger into the platform. Several theatrical films, including the Broly movies, are available for separate digital purchase or rental on most major platforms. Dragon Ball GT, considered non-canon by Toriyama and largely superseded by Dragon Ball Super, is less consistently available across streaming services than the other entries in the franchise.
