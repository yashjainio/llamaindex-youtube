Death Note — Series Overview

Overview
Death Note is a Japanese manga series written by Tsugumi Ohba and illustrated by Takeshi Obata, serialized in Weekly Shonen Jump from 2003 to 2006. Unlike most Shonen Jump series, it's a psychological thriller rather than an action-adventure story, centered on a supernatural notebook rather than martial arts or superpowers.

Protagonist and the Death Note's rules
Light Yagami, a brilliant high school student, discovers a black notebook dropped by a shinigami (death god) named Ryuk. The Death Note kills anyone whose name is written in it, as long as the writer knows the person's face. If no cause of death is specified, the person dies of a heart attack exactly 40 seconds after their name is written. The writer can specify a cause of death and details of the death, but only if it's written within 6 minutes and 40 seconds of writing the name, and the specified details must be physically possible. Light begins secretly using the notebook to kill criminals, adopting the alias "Kira" and styling himself as the architect of a new, crime-free world.

Story and central conflict
The core of Death Note is a cat-and-mouse investigation between Light and L, an eccentric, reclusive detective hired to identify and stop Kira. Much of the series' tension comes from both characters being extraordinarily intelligent and suspecting each other while maintaining a careful public alliance, each trying to expose the other without revealing their own hand. After L's storyline concludes, the series introduces his successors, Near and Mello, who continue the pursuit of Kira using different methods.

Production
Death Note was animated by Studio Madhouse and aired from 2006 to 2007, spanning 37 episodes that closely follow the manga's plot with relatively few filler additions, which is part of why the anime adaptation is often cited as one of the more faithful manga-to-anime adaptations in the genre.

Legacy and cultural impact
Death Note is widely regarded as one of the defining psychological thrillers in anime, frequently cited alongside Monster and Code Geass as an example of the genre done well. It popularized the "genius versus genius" narrative structure — two hyper-intelligent characters trying to outmaneuver each other through deduction rather than physical combat — which has influenced numerous subsequent series and is often referenced as a gateway series for viewers who don't typically watch anime.

Themes
Death Note interrogates the ethics of vigilante justice and the corrupting nature of unchecked power: Light begins his killing spree believing he's creating a better world, but the series steadily traces his descent from idealistic reformer to a paranoid, ruthless figure willing to kill anyone — including former allies — who threatens to expose him.

Supporting characters
Misa Amane, a pop idol who gains a second Death Note, becomes fiercely loyal to Light and complicates his plans as much as she assists them. Near and Mello, L's two chosen successors, take sharply different approaches to continuing the Kira investigation — Near favoring careful, methodical deduction, Mello favoring aggressive, high-risk tactics, including working alongside the mafia. Soichiro Yagami, Light's father and a senior police investigator, is torn between loyalty to his son and his duty to stop Kira, unaware for most of the series that the two are the same person.

Trivia
Death Note's rule that a name only works if the writer knows the target's face was specifically designed by the creators to prevent Light from simply killing everyone whose name he could learn, forcing the story to stay grounded in surveillance and deduction rather than indiscriminate killing. The series has been adapted into multiple live-action Japanese films, a Netflix live-action film, and a stage musical, though the original manga and 2006-2007 anime remain the most critically acclaimed versions.

Voice cast
Light Yagami is voiced by Mamoru Miyano in the original Japanese dub, a performance widely praised for tracking Light's gradual descent into megalomania. In the English dub produced by VIZ Media, Light is voiced by Brad Swaile. L is voiced by Kappei Yamaguchi in Japanese and Alessandro Juliani in English, both known for capturing the character's deliberately unusual, hunched posture and speech patterns even in voice-only performance.

Where to watch
Death Note is available on Crunchyroll and Netflix in most English-speaking territories, and the manga is available digitally through VIZ Media. Netflix also produced a live-action adaptation set in the United States, which diverges significantly from the source material's plot and is generally considered a separate, loosely connected interpretation rather than a faithful adaptation. A one-shot sequel chapter released years after the original series concluded briefly revisited the world, though it's treated by most fans as a standalone curiosity rather than a continuation of the main story's canon.
